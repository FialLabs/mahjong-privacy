Privacy policy

Tuilerie Mahjong asks nothing of you, and we keep nothing about you. Everything the game remembers stays on your phone. One thing talks to the network: Google's ad service, which starts with the game and sends Google what an ad service measures — four types of data, named below. No ad is ever shown unless you ask for one.

Who publishes Tuilerie Mahjong

Tuilerie Mahjong is developed by Fial Labs. For any question about this policy, or to exercise your rights: fiallabs@gmail.com

What Tuilerie Mahjong collects

Fial Labs collects nothing. The game does not ask for an account, a name, an email address or a phone number. It does not request your location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera, and we have installed no analytics tool, no crash reporter and no tracking code of our own.

Google's ad service, which the game embeds, does collect data. It starts when the game launches, whether or not you ever watch an ad, and it is the only third-party library in the app. Because of it, the Google Play listing shows four types of data as collected and shared. Here they are, under the names Play uses:

  • Device or other identifiers — your device's advertising identifier and its App Set ID;
  • Approximate location — derived from your IP address, not from GPS. The game never asks for Android's location permission and never reads your position; Google infers a region from the network address that every connection carries;
  • App interactions — the launch of the game, your taps in it, and the playback of ad videos;
  • Diagnostics — launch time, freezes, energy use.

All four go to Google and to nobody else, for advertising, analytics, and fraud prevention and security. They travel encrypted in transit (TLS). They are not optional inside the game: the ad service starts with it, and the only way to limit them is through your device's ad settings, described below. Google keeps them under its own policy; we never see them and receive no copy.

What Tuilerie Mahjong stores on your device

Everything is stored locally, in the app's private storage, and is never transmitted anywhere:

  • your settings — whether free tiles are highlighted, and the layout offered for a new board. The game also writes, at their starting value, four settings provided for but not yet offered on screen: language, hand, sound and vibration;
  • your stock of hints;
  • your statistics — games won, solved by the solver or abandoned, best time per layout, and the tile set you chose (tile sets unlock as you win games), plus two counters for the daily board, provided for but not yet used;
  • the game in progress — its number (which carries its layout), the moves played and their order, the time elapsed, the hints used and the reshuffles.

All of this disappears entirely if you uninstall the app or clear its data from Android's settings. We hold no copy of it, and we cannot restore it.

There is no recovery code

We would rather say this plainly, because it is a limitation and not a feature: Tuilerie Mahjong offers no way to carry your progress to another device. It lives on this phone and nowhere else. If you change device or clear the app's data, it is lost, and we can do nothing about it because we never had a copy.

The solver

The game checks that a game can be won, offers hints and can show a solution. All of that is computed on your phone. No position, no move and no game number is ever sent to a server: there is no server.

Advertising

Tuilerie Mahjong never forces an ad on you. No ad appears between games, at launch, or at any moment you did not choose. There are no interstitials at all — an automated check refuses to build the app if an interstitial is called anywhere in it. The game offers rewarded ads in one place only, and always at your request: the “Recharger” button, which tops up your stock of hints (the label is the same in every language). That button only exists on screen if an ad is actually loaded and your stock is below its ceiling; otherwise it does not appear, rather than promising you something that is not available. The stock also refills by winning games, with no ad at all.

These ads are served by Google AdMob. We would rather say it precisely: the ad service starts when the game launches, collects your consent where it is required, then loads an ad in advance so that it is ready if you ask for it. That loading contacts Google as soon as the game starts, even if you never watch an ad. On that occasion, and each time an ad plays, AdMob collects the four types of data listed above — identifiers, approximate location from your IP address, interactions, diagnostics — along with technical details such as device type and Android version, to select and measure the ads shown. Everything is sent over an encrypted connection (TLS) and processed by Google under its own privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy. We have no access to it and receive no copy of it.

Your consent and your ad preferences

If you live in the European Union, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, a consent form is shown to you at first launch, before any ad. It carries a “Do not consent” button as visible as the accept button. Where this choice is asked of you, the entry “Confidentialité des annonces” appears in the game's menu and lets you change it at any time.

On any Android device, you can also reset or delete your advertising identifier, or opt out of personalized advertising, from your device's settings (Settings › Google › Ads).

Android permissions

The game declares a single permission on its own behalf:

  • INTERNET — for the ad service: loading an ad in advance, and showing it when you ask. Nothing else in the game uses the network.

The AdMob library brings its own, which we prefer to name rather than hide: ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (whether the network is available), AD_ID and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_* (the advertising identifier and Android's advertising services), WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE (used by Google while an ad plays). None of these permissions is classed as “dangerous” by Android: they give access neither to your files, nor to your contacts, nor to your device's position. The approximate location Google records comes from your IP address, not from any of these permissions. One more is internal to the app, com.fiallabs.mahjong.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION, set by Android's androidx library so that its internal messages never leave the app.

What can be bought in the game

Nothing. There is no real-money purchase, no payment, and no banking data. The stock of hints refills by winning games, or through an ad if you choose. Tile sets unlock as you win games, never through an ad or a payment.

Transfers outside the European Union

Delete your data

This is the section Google Play links to under “delete data”. It names every piece of data that exists, where it lives, how to delete it, and what remains afterwards.

1. What is on your device — your settings, your stock of hints, your statistics and the game in progress. To delete all of it: uninstall the app, or open Android's Settings › Apps › Tuilerie Mahjong › Storage and choose “Clear storage”. Everything is deleted at once, and nothing of it is kept anywhere: there is no server and no copy at Fial Labs. We cannot restore it.

2. What Google's ad service holds — the four types listed above (identifiers, approximate location, interactions, diagnostics). We hold none of it and cannot delete it for you; Google can. On your device, Settings › Google › Ads lets you delete your advertising identifier, after which apps no longer receive it, or reset it, after which what was collected before is no longer tied to the new one. For the data Google keeps under its own policy, use Google's own tools at myaccount.google.com, or write to Google as described in its privacy policy.

3. What Fial Labs keeps — nothing. There is no account to close and no record to erase on our side, so there is nothing you need to ask us to delete. If you write to fiallabs@gmail.com to ask, we will confirm it in writing.
